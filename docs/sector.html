How picks and grades are made

Which table

Options: the monthly expiry nearest 30 days, ten contracts nearest the money, median bid/ask spread ≤ 10% and median open interest ≥ 100. Shares: options fail that, but the stock's median daily dollar volume over 20 sessions is ≥ $50M. Neither: not shown.

Quality gates

Negative free cash flow; gross or operating margin below the prior fiscal year; net debt above 3× EBITDA; estimated earnings within 14 days. A long must pass all of them. A short comes from names that fail at least one, weakest first.

Ranking

Composite = 2 × quality + momentum + 0.25 × value (+ 0.5 deep-value bonus). Quality = revenue growth + operating margin − net debt/EBITDA ÷ 10 − 0.5 per failed gate. Momentum = average 20- and 60-day return relative to the sector ETF. Shown: up to three longs and three shorts in the options table, the single best long and short in the shares table.

Valuation

Value = how cheap the stock is against its own sector, in standard deviations: price-to-book for financials; EV/EBITDA and free-cash-flow yield for the others. Below book value (P/B < 1) is never a short — deep value tends to range, not fall further. A deep-value stock (below book, or 1σ+ cheaper than its sector) that is improving — at least two of: operating margin up, gross margin up, revenue growing, beating its sector — gets a bonus as a long. Cheap and not improving gets nothing extra.

Watchlist

CPB is scored every run by exception, even below the liquidity screens or outside XLP’s top 30. Its rank is among every scorable name in its sector. It joins the ranked lists only if it qualifies on its own.

Entries from gamma walls (options table)

Each liquid name’s chain is run through the GEX Monitor’s own method (≤ 30 days, net dealer gamma by strike). The put wall is the strike below price where hedging tends to support it; the call wall, above, tends to cap it. Long: “at support” = within 2% above the put wall, the cleaner entry; “near cap” = within 2% of the call wall, little room — wait for a pullback. Short: “at resistance” = within 2% below the call wall; “near support” = wait for a bounce. +γ (price above the gamma flip) tends to range; −γ tends to trend. IV is at-the-money on the ~30-day monthly. Entry is shown, not scored. Beside each entry read: the nearest resistance (call wall) and support (put wall) strikes and their distance from price — the level being tested, and the one on the other side as a target or stop reference.

Wall strength is how little a wall has moved over the last ten sessions (one reading per session; a wall counts as held if it is within 1% of today’s level): strong — held 80%+ of the weighted sessions or 6+ in a row; steady — 50%+ or 3+ in a row; shifting — less; moved from X — it was at X last session, so today’s level is untested; new — under three sessions of history. Expirations: sessions before the latest monthly expiration count half, before a quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) a quarter — the expiring contracts built those walls. When an expiration falls in the window the tag also shows the record since it, e.g. “(2/2 since 10/16 exp)”.

Fundamentals basis

Quarterly: trailing four quarters against the four before when eight are reported, otherwise the latest quarter against the same quarter a year earlier; with only four, the trailing four quarters against the last full fiscal year. Fewer than four falls back to annual, tagged “annual data”. Quarterly figures are only as current as Yahoo’s feed.

Trade quality (0–100)

The bar under each name shows its four parts, left to right:

  • Conviction (30) — how far the composite stands from the sector average: 2 standard deviations in the trade's direction is full marks.
  • Momentum (25) — price confirms the side: +10% vs the sector over 20/60 days is full for a long, −10% for a short.
  • Execution (20) — options: tighter spread and deeper open interest; shares: daily dollar volume ($2B+ is full).
  • Event / carry (25) — earnings within 14 days costs half; a short also loses up to half for the dividend it pays (8%+ costs the half).
